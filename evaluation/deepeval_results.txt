QUESTION #1

What are the three Implementation Groups (IGs) defined in the CIS Controls, and how do they differ?

ANSWER:
The three Implementation Groups (IGs) defined in the CIS Controls are IG1, IG2, and IG3. IG1 is referred to as "essential cyber hygiene," representing the foundational set of cyber defense Safeguards that every enterprise should apply to guard against common attacks. IG2 builds upon IG1 by including all the Safeguards from IG1 plus additional ones, while IG3 includes all CIS Controls Safeguards in both IG1 and IG2, providing a comprehensive approach for enterprises with higher risk profiles and more resources.

QUESTION #13

Why do service providers pose a challenge to secure configuration of enterprise assets?

ANSWER:
Service providers often deliver default configurations that prioritize ease-of-deployment and ease-of-use over security, which can introduce weaknesses such as basic controls, open services and ports, default accounts or passwords, pre-configured DNS settings, older (vulnerable) protocols, and pre-installation of unnecessary software. These factors make it challenging for enterprises to maintain a secure configuration of their assets when using service providers' offerings.

What are the common ways attackers can covertly obtain access to user accounts?

ANSWER:

What is the main difference in focus between Control 5 (Account Management) and Control 6 (Access Control Management)?

ANSWER:
Control 5 focuses on managing the assignment of authorization to credentials for user accounts, including administrator and service accounts, while Control 6 specifically deals with ensuring users only have access to appropriate data or assets based on their role, emphasizing strong authentication for critical or sensitive functions.
